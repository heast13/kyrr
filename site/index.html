Just your voice.

kyrr is a small, free app for your microphone. Keyboard, fan and dog stay outside, and what you say gets typed into any app. All of it on your computer.

Download Source on GitHub

For Windows 10/11 and Linux. Free and open source.

kyrr (Old Norse): still, quiet, at rest.

What it does

Remove background noise

A neural filter works on your microphone in real time, for every app: Teams, Zoom, Discord, OBS, the browser. A large model for the cleanest result, a small one for older laptops.

Improve voice

A bit of polish once the noise is gone: a sound (from neutral to bright, or your own), a compressor, softer S sounds, pop protection and a cut for low rumble.

Automatic volume

Your voice arrives at the same level however loud the microphone is set. On from the start.

Dictation

Hold F9, talk, let go. The text lands at your cursor. German and English, transcribed on your computer. Say “new line” or “neue Zeile” for a line break.

Knobs, if you like knobs

The defaults are fine. If you want more, Settings has the sliders, and a check to see that it all works: a live level of what the microphone hears and what apps get, a ten-second test you can play before and after kyrr, and listening to yourself on headphones.

Private, as in: stays here

Your voice and your dictated text never leave your computer: no cloud, no account, no telemetry. After the one-time download of the speech model kyrr works offline; the only thing it does online on its own is a daily check for a new version, which you can switch off.

Read exactly what connects where

Download

Free, as in free. Pick your system.

Windows

Windows 10 22H2 or Windows 11, 64-bit.

Download kyrr-setup.exe

The installer needs administrator rights, and sound drops out for a second while kyrr sets up its filter. Until the installer is code-signed, Windows SmartScreen warns about it (“More info”, then “Run anyway”). Compare it with its .sha256 file on the release page first. Why it is not signed yet: code signing policy.

On Windows on ARM (Snapdragon and other ARM processors) kyrr only dictates: its noise filter is built for Intel and AMD.

Linux

One file for every distribution, an AppImage. 64-bit Intel or AMD with glibc 2.39 or newer: Ubuntu 24.04+, Fedora 40+, Debian 13, Arch. The noise filter needs AVX2 (most processors from 2015 on).

Download kyrr-x86_64.AppImage

Make it executable and start it:

chmod +x kyrr-x86_64.AppImage
./kyrr-x86_64.AppImage

Or in your file manager: right click, Properties, allow executing it as a program, then double click. On its first start kyrr adds itself to your app menu. To be sure, compare its SHA256 with the one on the release page.

Linux notes

  • PipeWire is required. It is the default on current Ubuntu, Fedora, Arch and Debian 12 and newer.
  • The “kyrr” microphone. kyrr adds a virtual microphone called “kyrr” and makes it your default. Apps that use the default microphone get the clean voice right away. Apps set to a specific microphone need to be switched to “kyrr”.
  • The dictation key goes through your desktop's global shortcut dialog (GNOME 48+, KDE Plasma). kyrr walks you through it the first time; F9 is already filled in. On Hyprland (Omarchy, say) kyrr shows the lines to add to your key bindings. On X11 the key just works. Anywhere else, put the command kyrr shows in its settings on a key in your desktop's keyboard settings.
  • Typing. Dictated text is typed into the focused window. The first time, GNOME or KDE asks whether kyrr may do that. Say yes; otherwise kyrr can only put the text on the clipboard.

Questions

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once the speech model is downloaded (once, 663 MB for English, 900 MB for German) kyrr works fully offline.

Do I need a graphics card?

No. kyrr runs on any 64-bit Intel or AMD PC. On an older laptop, choose the small noise model. On Linux the noise filter needs a processor with AVX2 (most from 2015 on); dictation works without.

Which languages does dictation understand?

German and English. The German model handles English too.

What does it cost?

Nothing. kyrr is free and open source, and there is no account to make.

How is it different from NVIDIA Broadcast or Krisp?

NVIDIA Broadcast needs an RTX graphics card. Krisp needs an account and is free for 60 minutes a day, then a subscription. Both work through a virtual microphone you pick in each app. kyrr needs neither; on Windows it sits on the microphone itself, so every app gets it with nothing to choose, and on Linux it becomes the default microphone. On top, it dictates into any app. As far as we know in October 2026; corrections welcome.
